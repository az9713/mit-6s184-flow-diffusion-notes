# Lecture-note production workflow

The applied skill stack is transcript-grounded-textbook, rigorous-explainer,
and science-editor. The exact MIT 6.S184 /2026/ course materials are the spine.
The user selected balanced mathematical foundations and runnable implementations,
and authorized separate author and reviewer subagents.

1. Calibrate against complete representative chapters from the named notes projects;
   inspect their source, rendering, validation, and editorial conventions.
2. Enumerate the exact course, recordings, official notes, slides, and three labs.
   Preserve raw sources privately, hashes, caption provenance and coverage limits.
3. Read each complete assigned transcript sequentially and map substantive topic
   changes to the chapter; check technical notation against official written sources.
4. Inspect primary research at the pages/sections used. Distinguish lecture
   material, researched extensions, and original mathematical teaching constructions.
5. Author substantive HTML sections with locally defined symbols, explicit theorem
   assumptions, proofs, interpreted equations, recurring examples, actual code
   results, and fully worked exercises. Maintain book-wide notation and continuity.
6. Send stable manuscripts to an independent scientific editor. Authors cannot
   accept their own work. Repair located findings and renew scientific review.
   Bind accepted manuscripts to exact authored-body SHA256 digests.
7. Audit parity, source chronology, first-use notation, citations and source
   inspection scope. Complete the full first-chapter sequence before scaling drafting.
8. Wrap authored bodies without modification; build offline navigation and math.
   Check links, timestamp bounds, source maps, equations, numerical programs,
   controls, desktop/mobile layout, and the final source-free reader package.
9. Record evidence and limitations separately. Keep private acquisitions,
   transcripts, PDFs, downloaded notebooks, and private editorial files outside
   this candidate public directory. Publication requires a separate user request.

A rendered page is not editorial acceptance. Numerical examples are synthetic
construction checks, not validation of a trained generative model or benchmarks.
