Learning route

The course moves from generating a random object to constructing a time-dependent law. Differential equations specify a sampler; conditional regression supplies a tractable training objective. Scores add stochastic sampling and conditioning. Latent representations reduce the state dimension, and Markov jump processes extend the construction to discrete objects.

Prerequisites are linear algebra, multivariable calculus, and probability. Brownian motion, stochastic integration, density evolution, and the model-specific training identities are developed where needed. The mathematical results state their regularity and endpoint assumptions; code checks examine finite synthetic models on a CPU.

Read each chapter's running construction, reproduce its calculation, and work the exercises before opening the solutions. The original three course labs provide a complementary PyTorch route. This edition's numerical checks support the derivations; their scope is recorded separately from the course's neural training exercises.

Keep the law, field, objective, and solver separate

A probability path specifies which distributions should occur over time. A transport velocity or stochastic drift specifies a mechanism for realizing that path. A regression objective supplies information from which a model can estimate the needed field. A numerical solver approximates the resulting dynamics. A correct result at one of these levels does not establish correctness at the others.

The first three chapters continue the same Gaussian endpoints while deliberately comparing different intermediate paths and samplers. The guidance chapter introduces paired Gaussian data to isolate conditioning. The latent and discrete chapters change the representation and state space. Each change is declared where it occurs; examples are mathematical constructions rather than measurements of an image generator.

Check understanding through mechanisms

Before moving on, reconstruct the conditional-expectation regression identity, the score correction in a stochastic drift, the guidance-scale convention, the variational lower-bound gap, and the generator's conservation equation. These are different mechanisms. Being able to reproduce them with their hypotheses is a stronger learning check than recognizing their names.
